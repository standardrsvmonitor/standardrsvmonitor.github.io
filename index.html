Revenue

Fees and revenue from the token economy, the reserve’s pools and the launchpad.

Revenue by stream, daily
iSTANDARD trading fees are the ETH tax the tax hook collects on every STANDARD trade and LP exit, dated by the block it was collected in. STANDARD pool LP fees are the 1% pool fee earned by the protocol’s own position in the STANDARD/ETH pool, replayed swap by swap at the share of active liquidity it held, both sides valued at the day’s close; the POL manager sends both to the team wallet when it harvests. LP fees replay every swap in the reserve’s pools tick by tick and value each swap’s fee at the pool price of that swap. The launchpad line is the protocol’s 70% of the 1% base fee plus snipe fees, received by the launchpad fee Safe, which sent its balance to the reserve Safe on 10 Nov 2026. Charter sales are recorded on the sale date. License sales are the STANDARD bankers pay for expansion licenses from their bank balances, burned on purchase, valued at the day’s closing price less the resolution fee in force that day, what the banker could have withdrawn instead.
Revenue by destination, trailing 30 days
iWhere revenue over the selected range went, the protocol’s own phrase being where fees land, followed to what it became. STANDARD trading fees and charter sales are routed at settlement: 70% to the reserve in expansion or the contraction vault in contraction, 15% to the POL buyback and 15% to the team. Each epoch’s routed ETH is then followed, first in, first out, through the chain: what the reserve paid for stock tokens, put into its pools or still holds as ETH and USDG, and ETH the reserve Safe sent to outside wallets with nothing back in the same transaction; STANDARD the contraction vault bought and burned; STANDARD the POL buyback bought for the Incentives vault, as the vault pays it to the S-Bill premium budget, to bankers through the per-branch claim and its top-ups, or to one other wallet, or still holds it; and routed ETH not yet spent on buybacks. Each UTC day’s fees take the shares of the epoch that starts at 01:00 UTC that day, so a settlement day’s first hour carries the next epoch’s shares. LP fees are earned in the reserve’s pools; the Safe collected them for the first time on 10 Nov 2026, and tokens back from positions count as principal first. STANDARD pool LP fees go to the team wallet. License sales are burned. The launchpad’s 70% and snipe fees arrive in the launchpad fee Safe; what the Safe has sent to the reserve Safe, oldest receipts first, counts in the reserve. Fees of the current epoch are not yet routed. Stock tokens are at cost. Stock tokens sent out of the reserve move to sent out: of each token’s purchase cost, the share of the reserve’s holding of that token (loose in the Safe and in its positions) that left, and launchpad tokens in full when the Safe’s loose balance left (all of its loose BABA and SKHY on 10 Nov 2026). The POL buyback also holds that no settlement routed to it; no event log shows its source, so it is left out.
Fee routing by epoch
iSTANDARD trading fees and charter proceeds routed by the fee engine at each settlement, in ETH, for the epochs settled inside the selected range. Green bars are expansion epochs, red contraction.
Price to revenue, daily
iMarket cap, price times circulating supply, over trailing revenue annualized: seven days at 7D, thirty days otherwise. Windows that include the launch epoch are omitted.

Liquidity

The reserve as market maker for stock tokens and ETH.

Protocol-owned liquidity by pool
iThe reserve’s position in each pool and its standing among every pool for the same asset on Robinhood Chain on the last complete day. Volume is the volume the reserve’s own liquidity served. APR is LP fees over the selected range, annualized over the average position value over the range. The fee is the rate the hook charged on the latest fee-paying swap; for the fixed-tier ETH/USDG pool, the tier its providers earn (each swap also pays Uniswap’s protocol fee, 0.0625% in all). Rank and venues count pools with at least $100 of liquidity; ETH/USDG’s chain standing is not shown (see the share of chain volume). LP fees were collected from every position for the first time on 10 Nov 2026.
Volume served by the reserve, by pool, daily
iEvery swap is replayed tick by tick against every provider’s liquidity; the reserve’s part of each step’s input, the fee included, is the volume its liquidity served, valued at the swap’s price. Other providers hold part of the liquidity in some pools; the reserve’s share is in the pools table. The headline is the volume that paid the pool fee. The stock pools’ hook keeps a fee-exemption list (FeeExemptSet): swaps sent by listed contracts pay no fee and show as fee-exempt traders. Its owner listed four contracts on 14 Oct 2026, removed three of them the same day, added a fifth on 21 Oct and listed the three again on 30 Oct, an arbitrage executor among them.
LP fees by pool, daily
iLP fees earned by the reserve’s positions. Every swap is replayed from the pool’s price before it to the price after it, one initialized tick at a time, against the liquidity of every provider rebuilt from the pool’s events; the fee on each step is charged on the input token and split among the providers in range, as the pool contract does. Each swap’s fee is valued at the pool price of that swap. The ETH/USDG figure excludes Uniswap’s 0.0125% protocol fee. A change to a position settles its accrued fees to the Safe: the 14 Oct 2026 addition to the ETH/USDG position settled 0.0097 ETH and 26.00 USDG, and the 3 Nov 2026 re-range of the BABA positions 2,614.69 USDG and 31.44 BABA, counted on the days they accrued. Each compile reconciles the fees not yet collected, at today’s pool price, with the same fees read from the positions; the difference from the sum of daily figures is the move in the pool price since each fee was earned.
Share of chain volume by pool, daily
iVolume served by the reserve’s liquidity as a share of all on-chain volume in the same asset, across every pool pairing the asset with USDG, ETH or WETH. Uniswap v4 pools are found on chain from the PoolManager’s Initialize events; those that held liquidity when the monitor read them, from 8 Nov 2026, are counted. Other pools are every pair DexScreener has listed. A v4 pool emptied before 8 Nov 2026 and never listed is left out, so chain volume is a floor and the share a ceiling. ETH/USDG is not shown: the venue list does not yet include the WETH/USDG pools, which are larger than the reserve’s (a single one traded $196M on 11 Nov 2026).
Share of chain liquidity by pool, daily
iThe reserve’s position value as a share of all on-chain liquidity in pools pairing the same asset with USDG, ETH or WETH, the same pools as the share of chain volume; ETH/USDG is not shown, for the same reason.
Pool volume by counterparty, daily
iFee-exempt swaps are summed exactly. The rest is estimated from 150 size-weighted trades per pool and day, read from receipts. Arbitrage trades the asset the other way on another pool in the same transaction. Organic users are wallets with fewer than 1,000 transactions trading through a public router. Other bots are everything else. Daily shares carry up to eight points of sampling error, seven-day shares about three.
Counterparty mix, trailing 30 days
iShare of the volume the reserve’s liquidity served in each pool over the selected range. Rows sum to 100%.
Pool profit and loss against holding, cumulative
iFees earned plus divergence, the position’s value less the value of holding the deposited tokens, since each pool was seeded. Position, holding and the fee tokens earned to date are all valued at the same day’s closing pool price.
Pool profit and loss since seeding
iNet against holding is fees plus divergence. Net in USD compares value plus fees with the USD cost of the deposits, so it also carries the move in the assets’ prices. Fees are the tokens earned to date at the last close, including those settled to the Safe. Hover a row for cost, value and holding.

Launchpad

Tokens launched against reserve markets, their trading and fees.

Tokens launched and trades, daily
iBars: tokens launched each UTC day, from the two launchpad factories’ launch events. Line: launchpad-token trades each day, on the bonding curve or in the graduated pool, from the Trade event every curve and launch hook emits, dated by block. Launchpad trading fell by about 99% after 4 Nov 18:00 UTC, when a trading competition the protocol ran on launchpad tokens, with a prize pool, ended; volume until then includes it.
Launched tokens by volume, trailing 30 days
iThe ten launched tokens with the most trading volume over the selected range of complete UTC days, from each token’s Trade events. Volume is the stock or WETH side of every trade. To creator is the creator’s part of each trade’s fee as the Trade event reports it: 30% of the base fee, more where the token sets a creator tax. Both are valued at each day’s closing price of the pair asset. Launch date and creator from the factory’s launch event; hover a row for the full symbol and the creator’s address.
Launchpad fees by recipient, daily
iEvery launchpad-token trade, on the bonding curve or in the graduated pool, pays a 1% base fee on its stock or WETH side: 30% to the token’s creator and 70% to a 2-of-4 Safe signed by the reserve’s signers, the protocol’s launchpad revenue; on 10 Nov 2026 at 15:06 UTC the Safe sent its whole balance to the reserve Safe. Curve buys in the first seconds after a launch also pay a snipe fee, all of it the protocol’s. Each trade’s split is read from the Trade event its curve or hook emits, dated by block. Creator receipts also include creator taxes and optional hook-module fees. Launches pay a fixed 0.0005 ETH to the fee Safe, not counted here. Valued at each day’s closing prices. Launchpad trading fell by about 99% after 4 Nov 18:00 UTC, when a trading competition the protocol ran on launchpad tokens, with a prize pool, ended; volume until then includes it.
Launchpad fee Safe, flows to date
iWhat the fee Safe received from launchpad trades (each trade’s Trade event, the same figure as launchpad revenue) and what it sent on (Transfer logs out of the Safe, oldest receipts first), both valued at the closing price of the day each receipt arrived; what it holds now at current prices. The token table is in token units, from Transfer logs.
LP fees from launchpad trades, daily
iLaunchpad trades paid in USDG or ETH cross the reserve’s stock pools on the way in and out. Every swap in the reserve’s pools executed by one of the launchpad’s two routers is counted exactly. Launchpad trades whose pool swap is sent by another contract (Uniswap’s router, an aggregator or another executor) are not identifiable from the swap and fall under all other trades, so the launchpad figure is a floor.

Token

Price, supply, burns, and who is buying and selling.

Price and market capitalization, daily
iDaily close: the protocol STANDARD/ETH pool’s own price after its last trade of the UTC day, at Coinbase’s ETH close; today is live. Market cap is price times circulating supply, every STANDARD minted, as the protocol’s site and DexScreener show it. Shaded days are contraction epochs.
Supply reconciliation
iFrom the genesis mint to circulating supply, the STANDARD in wallets and free float, at one block.
STANDARD supply, issued and burned, daily
iBars: STANDARD issued to bankers each day, the base issuance times the multiplier of the epoch in force (each epoch ties to the central bank’s own record at settlement), and STANDARD burned, token burns and ledger removals that are never minted. The line is circulating supply plus everything owed to bankers: ledger balances in banks and the stayers’ cut the bank holds before releasing it over the next epoch. STANDARD supply, 1B cap
iThe protocol’s own split of the one-billion hard cap, at the compile block. Protocol liquidity (STANDARD held by the Uniswap v4 PoolManager, the protocol’s own definition; about 77,000 of it sits in other STANDARD pools, not the protocol’s), in wallets and the Incentives vault make up circulating supply. In banks is owed to bankers and minted on withdrawal. Burned is the cap the protocol has removed: token burns and ledger removals that are never minted. Unissued is the rest of the maximum supply.
Net buying of STANDARD by holder group, daily
iSTANDARD bought from the protocol’s STANDARD/ETH pool less STANDARD sold into it, by the group of the wallet behind each trade. Above zero a group bought more than it sold; below zero it sold more. Groups apply in order: team wallet, current bankers (owners of at least one branch today), S-Bill holders (wallets that have ever deposited into S-Bills), frequent traders (300 or more trades with the pool), launch-epoch buyers (first buy within three days of genesis, the first epoch), others; a group with no trades is not drawn. A wallet’s group is its standing at the compile, so a wallet that later becomes a banker moves into Bankers for its whole history; each day’s total does not change.
Largest net sellers, trailing seven days
iNet STANDARD sold into the pool by wallet over the last seven complete days; trades through routers are assigned to the wallet behind the router.
STANDARD supply composition, daily
iThe protocol’s own split of circulating supply: in wallets, protocol liquidity and the Incentives vault, with the STANDARD in banks, ledger balances owed to bankers and minted only on withdrawal. The full height is every STANDARD that exists or is owed.

iDaily movements into and out of the STANDARD in wallets; the bars sum to the line.
STANDARD/ETH market depth, daily
iETH, in USD, the pool pays out to sellers taking STANDARD’s price down two and five percent (before the 3% sell tax), from every open liquidity range at the day’s closing price. Ask depth is in the tooltip.

Banks

Bank charters and branches, issuance, the Auction House and S-Bills.

STANDARD in banks, inflows and outflows, daily
iEvery movement of the STANDARD held in banks; each day’s bars sum to the change in the line. In: issuance, which every branch earns automatically, STANDARD bankers deposit from their wallets, and the stayers’ cut, half of every exit and revocation fee, paid to the bankers who stay as it streams out evenly over the epoch after the one it was collected in. Out: STANDARD spent on expansion licenses, which is burned, STANDARD withdrawn by closing branches (before the exit fee), and the balances of revoked charters. STANDARD in banks is ledger value, minted only when withdrawn.
Branches and active charters, daily
iA charter opens with one branch and licenses add up to nine more. Withdrawals and revocations destroy branches.
Charter auction clearing prices
iCharters are sold in 12-hour periods, in a Dutch auction that opens at three times the last clearing price (three times the floor after a period with no sale) and halves every two hours toward a 1 ETH floor. One charter a period until 11 Nov 2026, when the owner set the number to zero from the 23:25 UTC period. Triangles are purchases made through a contract.
License auction clearing prices and volume
iExpansion licenses are sold in 12-hour periods, paid in STANDARD and burned, opening at twice the last clearing price with a floor of about two days of one branch’s issuance. The owner sets the number a period and has changed it several times; 15 a period since 11 Nov 2026.
S-Bill principal and term rate, daily
iSTANDARD locked in 15-day S-Bills and the term rate at each day’s close.
Issuance multiplier and resolution fee, daily
iThe multiplier scales daily issuance of 700,000 STANDARD and moves at each epoch settlement within 0.20 to 1.25. The resolution fee, the exit fee on withdrawals, rises from 2% to 60% with seven-day exit pressure.
Top bankers
iRanked as the protocol’s own leaderboard ranks, by branches, which set each banker’s share of issuance, then by this epoch’s emissions. Share of emissions is the banker’s part of what this epoch has streamed to today’s bankers, issuance and the stayers’ cut; charters closed during the epoch are left out. Read from the protocol’s leaderboard right after the compile block. Names are the owners’ primary ENS names on Ethereum mainnet that resolve back to the owner. Rank change compares with the previous epoch’s close: the protocol records that rank with equal branch counts in address order, so the change orders today’s counts the same way and moves only when branch counts change.
